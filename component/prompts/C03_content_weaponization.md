## Prompt

You are helping me write a section of an academic survey on "Security and Safety of AI-Generated Content." This section (Section 3.1) covers how generative AI is being weaponized to produce malicious content. This section ONLY discusses threats and attacks — no defense solutions.

Please produce a comprehensive research document covering:

**1. MALICIOUS CONTENT GENERATION**

- AI-generated phishing emails: cite statistical evidence on increased click-through rates compared to human-crafted phishing, detection bypass rates against email security filters, and specific studies or reports quantifying the threat.
- AI-assisted malware generation: how LLMs produce obfuscated code, polymorphic malware, exploit code. Cite specific research demonstrating these capabilities and any documented real-world incidents.
- Weaponized LLMs: document the underground market for uncensored models — WormGPT, FraudGPT, DarkGPT, GhostGPT, and others. For each: when it appeared, what it offers, pricing if known, and how it bypasses safety guardrails (fine-tuned on malicious data vs. jailbroken wrapper).
- AI-generated forged documents: academic credentials, legal documents, fake news articles, fabricated financial reports.
- Provide 3–5 well-documented real-world cases from 2024–2025 with specific details (dates, amounts, outcomes, law enforcement actions).

**2. NON-CONSENSUAL SYNTHETIC CONTENT**

- AI-generated NCII (Non-Consensual Intimate Images): document the severity and scale of the problem. Cite specific statistics on volume, platforms affected, and demographic impact (disproportionate impact on women and minors).
- Targeted synthetic defamation: cases where AI-generated content was used to damage specific individuals' reputations.
- Legal and platform responses to NCII.
- Cite specific incidents, reports from organizations like NCMEC, Thorn, or government agencies.

For every claim, provide complete references — full paper/report titles, author names, publication venues, years, arXiv IDs, report URLs with access dates. Prioritize peer-reviewed sources and authoritative reports (government agencies, major cybersecurity firms, established research institutions).
