## Prompt

You are helping me write a section of an academic survey on "Security and Safety of AI-Generated Content." This section (Section 3.5) covers the ethical and societal risks. This requires nuanced, multi-perspective analysis rather than purely technical description.

Please produce a comprehensive research document covering:

**1. BIAS AMPLIFICATION**

- How training data biases are amplified in generative AI outputs. The mechanism: why generative models don't merely reflect but AMPLIFY existing biases.
- Specific documented examples:
  - Image generation biases: "CEO" = white male, occupational stereotyping, skin tone biases, cultural underrepresentation.
  - Text generation biases: gender, racial, and cultural biases in LLM outputs. Cite specific studies with quantitative findings.
  - Audio generation biases: accent and dialect representation.
- Evaluation benchmarks and methodologies: BOLD, BBQ, WinoBias, RealToxicityPrompts, and others. Describe what each measures.
- Feedback loops: how biased AI content, consumed and re-shared, further skews the information environment and potentially future training data.
- Intersectionality: how biases compound for individuals at the intersection of multiple marginalized identities.

**2. PSYCHOLOGICAL AND SOCIAL IMPACT**

- AI companions and emotional dependency:
  - Document the Character.ai incidents (teenager suicides linked to AI companion interactions) with appropriate sensitivity and factual accuracy.
  - The broader concern: parasocial relationships with AI systems.
  - Emerging research on psychological effects of long-term AI companion use.
- Adolescent-specific risks:
  - Exposure to harmful content generated on demand.
  - Identity confusion and developmental impact.
  - Age verification challenges.
- Creator displacement and economic anxiety:
  - Impact on artists, writers, musicians, voice actors, translators.
  - Cite specific industry data: job market changes, income impact, creative industry responses.
  - The philosophical dimension: what happens to human creative expression when AI can produce "good enough" content?
- Mental health implications of living in an environment of pervasive synthetic content.

**3. COPYRIGHT, AUTHENTICITY, AND ACCOUNTABILITY**

- Training data copyright litigation:
  - NYT v. OpenAI — status, key arguments, implications.
  - Getty Images v. Stability AI — status, key arguments.
  - Other significant cases: authors' lawsuits, music industry cases, code (GitHub Copilot litigation).
  - Summarize the core legal tension: is training on copyrighted data "fair use" / "transformative use"?
- AI-generated content copyright attribution:
  - US Copyright Office position: "no human author = no copyright."
  - Other jurisdictions' positions (EU, UK, China, Japan).
  - The "Zarya of the Dawn" case and its implications.
  - Practical implications for creators using AI tools.
- Authenticity crisis:
  - Journalism: how AI-generated content undermines journalistic credibility.
  - Judiciary: admissibility of AI-potentially-generated evidence.
  - Academia: AI-generated papers, theses, and the integrity of scholarly record.
- The accountability dilemma:
  - When AI-generated content causes harm, who is liable? Developer vs. deployer vs. end-user.
  - Current legal frameworks and their gaps.
  - The "distributed responsibility" problem.

Provide complete references — full paper titles, author names, publication venues, years, case docket numbers for legal cases, URLs with access dates. Handle sensitive topics (NCII, adolescent harm, suicide) with appropriate academic rigor and sensitivity.
